\subsection{RQ1: transfer against the baselines}
Table~\ref{tab:rq1} reports, for each method's best configuration, how many held-out instances one frozen rule list
certifies. On the random grids, a single rule list written by Haiku certifies 6.25 of the 8 validation grids (five
runs, 5.5--7.0); Sonnet certifies 7.25. The hand-written template certifies 6; decision trees learned from optimal
strategies certify none over raw coordinates, as in 1--2--3--Go!, and 4.0 when they are given our features; the
search over the same rule language certifies 4.5. Without the shortest-path feature, Haiku's lists certify 3.4,
against 1.5 for the trees and 1.25 for the search.

On the families no baseline is good everywhere: trees over our features certify 19 of 20 crossing members but 2
rooms members, and PPO over our features 19 rooms members but no crossing member. Sonnet's lists certify 19, 9 and
17; Haiku's 8.4, 4.6 and 16.0, with large differences between runs.

\paragraph*{A library of lists}
Certification at run time changes the picture (Section~\ref{sec:approach-library}). On the random grids, the four
lists of any single Haiku run together certify all 8 validation grids, in each of the five runs; the four
training-set lists of the search or of the trees together certify 7. On the families, three Haiku runs together
certify 42.6 of the 60 validation members on average (32--50 depending on the runs), and all five runs 50, against
37 for the best single baseline. \todo{baseline libraries on the families: PPO with several seeds.}

\subsection{RQ2: what makes the rules transfer}
\todo{Figure: the ablation dot plot.} Changes to the prompt do not move transfer: saying that the rules will be
certified on unseen larger instances, asking for the strategy in words first, explaining how open choices are
resolved, feedback in feature space, hints and different training sets all give 5.3--6.25 of 8 grids, within the
spread between runs. Resolving the LLM's open choices by verification does: in the loop, it is what lets rules
transfer without the shortest-path feature (0 to 3.4 of 8) and pass the crossing family at all. A fixed action order
known to the LLM does as well on the grids (7.17) but fails on crossing (1.3), where the right order depends on the
family. \todo{hidden members, counterexamples, traces, polishing.}

\subsection{RQ3: cost and size}
A Haiku training set costs about one cent (31--50k input and 6--15k output tokens), a Sonnet one about 60 cents. A
frozen list has 5--10 conditions (20--40 rules once each open choice is written out per action), against
\todo{X} nodes for the trees and a 64-by-64 network for PPO.
