\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph*{Policies for parameterised MDPs}
1--2--3--Go!~\cite{azeem2025onetwothreego} learns a decision tree from the optimal strategies of small instances of a
parameterised MDP and computes its value on each larger one. It targets near-optimality for one reachability
objective, and its predicates compare raw state variables with constants, so it cannot follow parameters that move
with the instance; its authors report failures on Pac-Man for this reason. We compare against it directly, and
against a variant that sees our features.

\paragraph*{Families of MDPs}
Policy trees~\cite{andriushchenko2024trees} and satisfiability modulo probabilistic model
checking~\cite{heck2026smpmc} synthesise policies that are provably correct for every member of a finite family, but
the members share one state space, so the policies do not reach larger instances.

\paragraph*{Generalized policies for probabilistic planning}
Relational decision lists~\cite{fern2006api,yoon2002inductive}, ASNets~\cite{toyer2020asnets} and
SymNet~\cite{garg2020symnet} learn one policy for a planning domain from small instances and apply it to larger ones;
they are evaluated by simulation. Reinforcement learning on procedurally generated levels (e.g.\ MiniGrid) learns
policies across a distribution of environments, also without guarantees. Our rule lists are first-match decision
lists like those of Fern et al.; what differs is who writes them and what certifies them.

\paragraph*{LLM-written generalized plans}
LLMs write generalized plans and policies as programs for classical planning
domains~\cite{silver2024generalized,stein2026strategy}, refined from test failures; in a deterministic domain, running
the plan checks it. In a stochastic one it takes model checking, and we check several probabilistic requirements at
once on every instance.

\paragraph*{Permissive strategies and shields}
Rules that leave choices open are permissive strategies~\cite{drager2015permissive}; we claim soundness (every allowed
policy meets every requirement), not maximal permissiveness.
